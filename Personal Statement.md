**Personal Statement — MA Education (Leadership and Management), Brunel University London**

My academic and professional journey in software engineering has increasingly become connected to a different educational imperative: helping institutions turn educational innovation into sustainable practice. Growing up in Bahir Dar, Ethiopia, I witnessed how unequal access to resources could limit educational opportunity, which first led me to explore how technology could widen access. My experience in education has since taught me that providing technology is only part of the solution. Lasting improvement depends on the people who use it and the institutions that support it. I am therefore convinced that pursuing the MA Education (Leadership and Management) at Brunel University London will be a crucial step in developing my ability to lead sustainable educational change in Ethiopia.

My time at Teach For Ethiopia, a non-governmental organisation, provided practical insight into the challenges of educational change. I led the establishment of computer laboratories in five public schools, providing access to over 250 students and training 30 educators through targeted computer-literacy activities. I also led a literacy-boost programme, organised reading camps, facilitated community activities based on action research, and organised fundraising for a summer programme. While working on these initiatives, I encountered frequent power interruptions, poor internet availability and challenges in encouraging sustained use of new resources. These experiences made me realise that educational innovation cannot be sustained by technology or resources alone; it requires leadership, coordination, ownership and the ability to bring people together around a shared purpose. As a university lecturer, I have strengthened this understanding by teaching undergraduate and extension courses, guiding internships and research projects, advising students, participating in departmental governance and conducting research.

I am particularly drawn to Brunel's Education MA because it can prepare me to understand these challenges beyond my own professional experience. The **Leadership and Management pathway** directly reflects the area I now want to develop, while the wider programme provides the theoretical and methodological foundation needed to understand educational change critically. The **Theory and Methodology of Educational Research** study block will strengthen my ability to investigate educational problems systematically and evaluate evidence, while **Comparative and International Education** will allow me to examine how educational leadership and practice are shaped by different national and institutional contexts. I am also attracted to the programme's dissertation because it will enable me to investigate an educational leadership question arising from my professional experience. Brunel's emphasis on educational theory, contemporary educational questions, global perspectives and independent research closely matches the skills I now need to develop.

Upon completion of the MA, I plan to return to Ethiopia and build on my experience in schools and higher education by contributing to educational improvement programmes that strengthen local leadership capacity. I aim to develop an initiative focused on helping educators and institutional leaders sustain technology-supported educational programmes in resource-constrained settings, with an initial focus on schools and universities in the Amhara region. Rather than concentrating only on providing resources, the initiative would support leaders in planning implementation, coordinating responsibilities, developing local ownership and evaluating whether innovations become part of normal institutional practice. In the longer term, I aim to progress into roles where I can contribute to educational programme leadership, institutional development and evidence-based education policy in Ethiopia, while continuing to conduct practitioner-focused research.

This opportunity represents a professional turning point because it would allow me to connect three parts of my experience that have so far developed separately: the systems thinking I gained from Software Engineering, the direct educational experience I gained through Teach For Ethiopia, and the teaching, research and governance responsibilities I have undertaken in higher education. Brunel's MA Education (Leadership and Management) would give me the theoretical knowledge, research capability and wider educational perspective to bring these experiences together. My goal is not simply to introduce educational innovations, but to develop the leadership and institutional capacity required to make those innovations sustainable.
